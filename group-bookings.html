What to Send for a Group Quote

  • ✓ Preferred trip dates or date range
  • ✓ Expected guest count and age mix
  • ✓ Whether you want snorkeling, diving, or a mixed plan
  • ✓ Whether you need one boat or multiple departures
  • ✓ Any resort pickup, meal, or coordination support required

How group planning usually works

1. We size the departure plan

We look at your guest count, activity mix, and whether one shared launch window works or multiple boats are needed.

2. We match the support level

Large groups often need separate beginner briefings, extra coordinators, and a clearer split between divers and snorkelers.

3. We confirm timing and logistics

Once the shape of the trip is clear, we lock the reporting window, payment flow, and any Murudeshwar-side coordination details.

What usually affects the quote

  • ✓ Total guest count and whether everyone is on the same activity plan
  • ✓ Number of boats and instructors required for the group mix
  • ✓ Whether meals, pickup support, or resort coordination are part of the plan
  • ✓ How much notice is available before the preferred departure date

Sample group formats we usually plan

College adventure day

Often built around a large snorkeling base with a smaller scuba segment, clear reporting instructions, and enough briefing support for first-timers.

Corporate or resort outing

Usually needs smoother arrival coordination, a simpler communication chain, and realistic timing buffers so the trip does not collide with check-in or event schedules.

Family cluster with mixed ages

Usually works best with a calmer pace, boat-size matching, and an activity split that does not force every guest into the same comfort level.

Smaller parties that mainly want schedule control may fit the private boat page better than a full group-coordination quote.

Large-group planning usually succeeds or fails on information quality. The earlier you can confirm the guest count range, the activity split, and whether everyone needs the same departure, the easier it is to size boats and staffing without turning the quote into guesswork.

What usually slows a large-group booking down

Guest count keeps moving

Boat sizing is harder when the headcount is still changing every day, especially if the plan mixes scuba and snorkeling.

No one owns the final decision

Groups move faster when one organizer can lock the date, payment flow, and reporting instructions instead of routing every answer through multiple people.

Transport and activity are planned separately

The trip feels smoother when the road, room, and departure timing are planned as one sequence instead of three unrelated decisions.

The smoother the organizing side is, the easier it is to protect the actual guest experience on the water.

Good coordination creates a calmer briefing, cleaner boarding, and fewer surprises once the boats are loaded.
