Netrani

A heart-shaped island of rock and fig forest, nineteen kilometres off Murudeshwar, and the reef that lives around it.

01 · The island

From the shore, a shape on the horizon.

On clear evenings you can pick Netrani out from Murudeshwar beach, a dark hump against the sunset. Up close it is steep rock rising straight out of the sea, with no beach and no jetty. A crown of fig forest sits on top, white water breaks all the way round, and nobody lives there.

03 · The crossing

Nineteen kilometres of open sea.

Netrani lies about 19 km (10 nautical miles) west-southwest of Murudeshwar’s temple. Every trip starts on the mainland, on the beach beside the temple headland.

Keep scrolling to make the crossing.

Leaving Murudeshwar

Early boats, and a giant watching them go.

Dive boats leave from the beach in the early morning, when the sea is calmest. Behind you on Kanduka hill sits the 123-ft (37 m) Shiva statue, facing the Raja Gopura, a 20-storey temple tower about 249 ft (76 m) tall, with the sea at its back.

The headland is laid out from OpenStreetMap footprints; buildings and statues are simplified.

Open water

Forty-five minutes to an hour and a half.

It depends on the boat and the sea. The mainland sinks into the haze behind you, and on a clear day Netrani shows up as a dark shape on the horizon long before you can make out its cliffs. The map in the corner tracks the boat.

Arrival

The boat stops off the reef, never at the island.

There is no jetty and no beach. Tourists don’t land: the crew drops anchor over the reef, and the dive begins from here.

Getting in

Backwards over the side.

Kitted up, divers sit on the edge of the boat with their backs to the sea. One palm holds the mask and regulator in place, the other hand holds the mask strap behind the head, and they roll backwards, cylinder first. The guide goes in first and waits for the others.

At the surface they inflate their jackets and signal “OK” with a hand on top of the head, big enough for the boat to see.

Descent

Down the line, ears first.

Divers let the air out of their jackets and sink slowly, feet first, a hand on the anchor line to control the speed. Every half metre or so they pinch their nose and blow gently to equalise their ears, before it hurts, not after. The line leads straight down to the reef.

04 · Beneath the surface

Below the surf line, the island keeps going.

The rock carries on underwater: boulders, then reef crusted with coral, then sand, down to about 40 m. When scientists first surveyed it in May 2006 they counted 14 kinds of coral and 92 kinds of fish. The gauge shows your depth; watch the pressure climb and the colours drain.

This team are certified divers, down to about 16 m here. Try dives stay shallower, above 12 m.

Bottom time

Forty minutes, not forty seconds.

A dive here lasts 30 minutes to an hour, set by the air in the cylinder and the time limits for the depth. The dive computer in the corner keeps real time while you scroll: watch the minutes add up and the cylinder drain from 200 bar. The guide turns the group back with plenty of air to spare.

05 · Light

Red goes first.

Water absorbs long wavelengths fastest. A few metres down, reds turn brown. By about ten metres, orange fades too, and the world turns blue-green. Watch the guide’s torch: its light has only a short way to travel, so in its beam the reef’s reds and oranges come back. The colour was there all along.

Sweep your cursor through a shoal and watch it scatter.Tap the water to scatter a shoal. Every diver’s bubbles swell as they rise: less pressure, more volume.

Out in the blue

Sometimes, something enormous passes.

On 10 May 2006, scientists surveying the reef logged a juvenile whale shark, the largest fish in the sea. Whale sharks are harmless plankton-feeders, and no trip can promise one.

Ascent

Up slowly, and a pause at five metres.

The group comes back up the line slowly, about 9 m a minute. At 5 m they hang for three minutes, a safety stop that gives the body time to shed nitrogen taken up at depth. Then the boat, the sun, and the long ride home.

06 · The cutaway

Now lift the sea away.

Here is the whole place as one block: the island, the reef, and the water column, sliced open like a sample on a lab bench.

Field guide

Who you might meet.

Reef regulars, lucky-day visitors and the island’s namesakes. Netrani is a living reef, not an aquarium, so sightings are never scheduled.
