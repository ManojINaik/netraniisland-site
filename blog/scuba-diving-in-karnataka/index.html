Scuba Diving in Karnataka: Where to Dive and Why Netrani Leads

By Netrani Desk · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answers

  • Netrani Island, about 19 km off Murudeshwar, is the state's main coral reef dive and takes first-timers.
  • Kapu near Udupi has shallow, rocky dives with 5 to 10 m visibility, according to Karnataka Tourism.
  • The islands off Karwar have smaller snorkelling and diving trips; St Mary's Island near Malpe is known for basalt rocks, not reef.
  • From Bengaluru it is about 500 km to Murudeshwar; travel overnight and dive after a day there.
  • The season runs roughly October to May; coastal districts stop sea activities in the monsoon.

When people talk about scuba diving in Karnataka, they almost always mean Netrani Island, a small rocky island about 19 km off Murudeshwar in Uttara Kannada district. It is the state's main coral reef dive. Scientists have surveyed its reef several times since 2006, the water is clear for 15 to 30 m on good days, and beginners can do a guided try dive there without a certificate.

There are a few smaller options along the coast: shallow dives off Kapu near Udupi, and snorkelling and some diving around the islands off Karwar. Both are smaller scenes than Netrani. If you are coming from Bengaluru, plan on about 500 km to Murudeshwar, an overnight journey and a day there before the dive.

The season runs roughly from October to May. In the monsoon, the coastal districts stop sea activities.

Why is Netrani the main reef dive?

Netrani has the reef, and the science behind it. A team from the Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute dived there in May 2006 and recorded 14 coral species and 92 fish species. They also found the humphead wrasse, an endangered fish, and a giant clam that was the first record of its kind from mainland Indian waters. Later studies added more corals and fish to the list. A Times of India report from Karwar in 2018 put it plainly: apart from Netrani, there is no coral reef anywhere in Uttara Kannada district.

The diving is also regulated. The Uttara Kannada district administration permits a limited number of dive agencies at Netrani and caps how many divers go out each day; in 2020 it decided to raise that cap from 60 to 100 divers a day, The Hindu reported. Karnataka Tourism promotes Netrani for scuba and snorkelling, and the state backed a Netrani scuba diving festival in January 2018.

The sea around the island ranges from about 6 m to 40 m deep, so there is shallow reef for a first try and deeper water for certified divers. Landing on the island is not allowed, so every dive is made from a boat anchored nearby. What about Kapu, Karwar and Malpe?

Kapu (Kaup), near Udupi. Karnataka Tourism describes Kapu as a budding dive spot with shallow, rocky reef, coral patches and small reef fish. It puts visibility at 5 to 10 m depending on the season and the tide, and the best months at November to March. It also says Kapu is not as deep or as extensive as Netrani, so think of it as a short, shallow introduction close to Udupi and Manipal.

The islands off Karwar. In 2017 the Uttara Kannada administration identified Devgad and Kurumgad islands off Karwar for diving, and by 2018 it had started scuba events near Devgad, where corals have grown on a merchant ship that sank there in 2008. Karnataka Tourism lists snorkelling and diving among Devbagh's activities. It is a smaller scene than Netrani, so check what is operating before you build a trip around it.

St Mary's Island, off Malpe. St Mary's is known for its six-sided basalt columns, declared a national geological monument by the Geological Survey of India in 1979. Scuba, cliff diving and slacklining were introduced there for a month on a trial basis in January 2023. When water sports reopened at Malpe beach in September 2026, the list included jet skis, parasailing, banana rides and boat rounds, but not scuba. Go for the rocks and the boat ride, not for reef diving.

Which spot suits your trip?

  • First dive, coming from Bengaluru or further away: Netrani. It has the reef, 15 to 30 m visibility on good days and boats running through the season.
  • Staying in Udupi or Manipal with only half a day: Kapu is closer, but expect shallow water and 5 to 10 m visibility.
  • On holiday in Karwar: ask locally whether trips are running around Devbagh and Kurumgad, and keep Netrani for a proper reef dive.
  • Want a certificate: plan a few days at Netrani in season, or start with pool sessions in Bengaluru and finish at sea.

Can you learn scuba in Bengaluru?

You can make a start. A few dive centres in Bengaluru run pool try-dives and the classroom and pool part of courses, so you reach the coast already used to the gear. There are no fish in a pool, though, and the open-water dives still happen at sea; Netrani is one of the places those courses finish.

If you are already on the coast, you can also learn at Netrani itself during the season.

How do you plan a Netrani dive from Bengaluru?

Murudeshwar is about 500 km from Bengaluru by road, and the dive boats leave early, so the reliable plan is to travel overnight, spend a day in Murudeshwar and dive the following morning.

  • Train: the daily Panchaganga Express (16595) leaves KSR Bengaluru at 6:50 PM and reaches Murdeshwar station on the Konkan Railway at about 6:04 AM. Timetables change, so check on IRCTC before you book.
  • Bus: KSRTC and private sleeper buses run overnight from Bengaluru to Murudeshwar.
  • Car: expect about 9 to 11 hours of driving, so rest a night before the boat.
  • Air: Mangaluru airport is about 160 km from Murudeshwar by road, so fly in the day before.

When is the diving season in Karnataka?

Roughly October to May. Every monsoon the coastal districts stop sea water sports, and the exact dates change from year to year, so check before an early or late-season trip. Our guide to the best time to visit Netrani goes through the season in detail.

Questions travellers ask

Where is the main scuba diving spot in Karnataka?

Netrani Island, also called Pigeon Island, about 19 km off Murudeshwar in Uttara Kannada district. Boats leave from Murudeshwar beach in the October to May season, and first-timers can do a guided try dive without a certificate.

Is there scuba diving near Bangalore?

Not in the sea. Bengaluru has pool sessions, but for a reef dive you travel about 500 km to Murudeshwar and take the boat out to Netrani. Most people go overnight and dive after a day in town; see Bangalore to Netrani Island.

Is there scuba diving in Gokarna?

Scuba trips sold in Gokarna generally go to Netrani, leaving from Murudeshwar further south. Boats leave early, so either stay in Murudeshwar the night before or set out from Gokarna well before dawn.

Do I need to know swimming for a try dive in Karnataka?

No. A try dive has no swim test; what counts is being relaxed in water and able to float at the surface, and the crew judges that on the day. A full certification course is different and does include a swim test.

When is scuba diving closed in Karnataka?

During the monsoon. In Uttara Kannada the 2025 ban on sea water sports ran from 2 June to 30 September and the 2026 ban from 1 June to 31 August, so the dates change every year. At Malpe in Udupi, boat services and water sports stop from 16 May to 15 September.

Can I get a scuba certificate in Karnataka?

Yes. Courses such as PADI Open Water can be done at Netrani in season. They take a few days and include a swim test of 200 m, or 300 m with mask, fins and snorkel, so ask the desk about course dates when you enquire.
