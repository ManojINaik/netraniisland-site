Murudeshwar beach, right beside the temple, has five quick water rides in season: jet ski, banana boat, speed boat, kayak and parasailing (many people call it paragliding). Each costs between ₹200 and ₹1,500, lasts 5 to 30 minutes and is paid at the counter on the beach.

The bigger trip on the water is the boat to Netrani Island, about 19 km offshore, for snorkelling and scuba diving. That one leaves early in the morning, runs only when the sea allows and needs booking. Snorkelling trips start from ₹2,200 per guest; scuba is quoted for your date.

What is each ride like?

Jet ski

A fast loop of five to seven minutes on a water scooter, with spray in your face and plenty of bumps. Ask at the counter whether you drive or sit behind the operator's rider, as it varies. It is the most intense of the beach rides.

Banana boat

A long inflatable tube towed by a speed boat, with a group sitting astride it. The ride is bumpy and often ends with everyone tipped into the water, which is the point for most people. It suits friends and older children who are happy to get wet.

Speed boat

A 15 to 20 minute run along the coast. The price is per boat, so a family shares one fare, and you see the temple tower and the Shiva statue from the sea. Choose this if elders or small children want to be on the water without getting soaked.

Kayaking

Half an hour of paddling close to the shore at your own pace. It is the quietest ride on the beach, and the water is usually flattest in the morning.

Parasailing

You are harnessed to a parachute towed by a speedboat and lifted high above the sea. Karnataka Tourism's page on parasailing at Murudeshwar puts the height at up to 300 feet and describes both a dry take-off and a version with a quick dip in the water.

It runs only when the wind is right: mainly November to February on our sister site's list, while Karnataka Tourism says it is usually available between October and March. Weight and age limits are set by the operator on the day, so ask before you queue.

When do the rides run?

  • Season: roughly October to May, on days when the sea is calm enough. Rides stop when the sea turns rough, and parasailing waits for the right wind.
  • Monsoon: every year the district administration bans water adventure activities, boating and scuba diving included. In 2026 the ban ran from 1 June to 31 August, and beach rides and Netrani boats both stopped.
  • Crowds: weekends, long weekends and Christmas to New Year are the busiest, with queues at the counters. Weekday mornings are usually quieter.

Can you do beach rides and Netrani on the same trip?

Yes, and most people do, but not in the same morning. The Netrani boat takes your morning: you report early, cross for 45 to 90 minutes each way and are back on the beach in the afternoon.

A simple way to fit both in:

  1. Arrival day: check in, visit the temple, and spend the late afternoon on beach rides and the sunset.
  2. Netrani day: the boat in the morning; after you return, rest, eat and keep the evening easy.
  3. Departure day, if you have one: a kayak or speed boat ride in the morning before you leave.

Which boat ride do you want?

Boating in Murudeshwar can mean three quite different things:

  • A speed boat ride from the beach: 15 to 20 minutes along the coast, ₹1,500 per boat at the January 2026 price, no booking needed.
  • A shared Netrani trip: a half-day outing to the island with snorkelling from ₹2,200 per guest, or a try dive quoted for your date. If you are torn between the two, read snorkelling vs scuba at Netrani.
  • A private boat to Netrani: from ₹18,000 for up to 6 guests, for families and friends who want their own boat and timing. See private boat booking.

There is no public ferry to Netrani, and visitors do not land on the island. Every trip anchors nearby and returns the same day; the boat timings page explains the morning window.

How do you stay safe on the beach rides?

  • Wear the life jacket on every ride, fastened, even on the speed boat.
  • No alcohol before a ride; operators can refuse service.
  • If you are pregnant or have a heart condition, skip the jerky rides such as the jet ski and the banana boat.
  • Leave your phone and wallet at the hotel, or in a dry bag with someone on the sand.
  • Do not ride when the sea is rough or when a lifeguard or the operator says no.

Questions travellers ask

How much does parasailing cost in Murudeshwar?

Parasailing was listed at ₹800–1,000 for a 5 to 10 minute flight in January 2026 on our sister site. Prices change, and the ride runs only when the wind allows, so check at the beach counter on the day.

Can we swim in Murudeshwar beach?

You can paddle at the edge, but this is open sea and the current can be strong on rough days. Lifeguards watch the main beach: stay where they allow and come out when they signal, because people have drowned here after ignoring their warnings. Never go in during the monsoon or after dark.

Is boating available in Murudeshwar?

Yes. Speed boat rides of 15 to 20 minutes leave from the beach, at ₹1,500 per boat in January 2026, and boats to Netrani Island leave in the mornings through the season when the sea allows. Netrani trips start from ₹2,200 per guest for snorkelling, or ₹18,000 for a private boat.

Do we have scuba diving in Murudeshwar?

Yes, at Netrani Island, about 19 km offshore by boat. Beginners do a try dive with an instructor after a briefing and a medical questionnaire, and the price is quoted for your date. Start with our Netrani scuba diving guide.

Are water sports open in Murudeshwar during the monsoon?

No. Each monsoon the Uttara Kannada district administration bans water adventure activities, including boating and scuba diving; in 2026 the ban ran from 1 June to 31 August. Rides and Netrani boats usually restart by October, once the sea settles.

Do I need to book the beach rides in advance?

Usually not. Jet ski, banana boat, speed boat, kayak and parasailing are bought at the counter on the beach. The Netrani boat is different: it leaves early with a fixed number of seats, so book it ahead.

Is there a weight or age limit for parasailing?

Limits are set by the operator on the day and depend on the harness, the boat and the wind. Ask at the counter before you queue, especially for children.
