Netrani Scuba Diving Age Limit: Kids, Seniors and Non-Swimmers

By Netrani Desk · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

  • Over 45, you answer extra questions on smoking, cholesterol, blood pressure and family heart history.

The usual minimum age for scuba diving at Netrani Island is 10. That is PADI's minimum for a Discover Scuba try dive and for its Junior Open Water course, and Karnataka Tourism gives the same age for Netrani, subject to each operator's policy. Operators can set stricter limits of their own, so tell us every diver's age when you enquire.

There is no upper age limit in PADI's rules. What decides it, at any age, is the diver medical questionnaire everyone fills in before diving. Answer no to every question and you can dive; some yes answers mean a doctor has to sign you off first. You do not need to be a swimmer, but you do need to be comfortable in water.

What can children aged 10 to 14 do?

At 10, a child can do the same try dive as an adult, to a maximum of 12 m, with an instructor beside them. PADI is stricter about the youngest divers: any open-water dive that includes 10 or 11 year olds is limited to four divers per instructor, and no more than two of the four can be that age. A parent or guardian signs the medical form.

Age is only the minimum. PADI's instructor manual tells instructors to judge whether a particular 10-year-old is ready. A child who is scared, cannot clear their ears or does not follow the briefing should not be pushed, and a good instructor will stop the dive. Talk it through with your child beforehand, and make it clear they can say no.

If your child wants to learn properly, the Junior Open Water course starts at 10 and includes a swim test: 200 m without aids, or 300 m with mask, fins and snorkel. Once certified, 10 and 11 year olds dive to 12 m with a parent, guardian or PADI professional; from 12 to 14 the limit rises to 18 m with any certified adult.

Is there an upper age limit for scuba diving?

Not in PADI's rules, so a healthy 60 or 70 year old can try. The medical questionnaire does ask more of you after 45. Over that age, you also answer whether you smoke or use nicotine, have high cholesterol or high blood pressure, or have had a close relative die suddenly or of heart disease or stroke before 50. A yes to any of those means a doctor's evaluation before you dive.

Everyone, at any age, is also asked whether they struggle with moderate exercise, such as walking 1.6 km in 14 minutes or swimming 200 m without resting; a yes there also needs a doctor. Heart health is the reason behind these questions, and our guide to whether scuba diving is safe at Netrani explains why it matters. Prescription medicines also need a doctor's sign-off, except birth control and anti-malarials other than mefloquine, so bring a list of what you take.

Which health conditions need a doctor first?

The diver medical questionnaire that PADI uses sends you to a doctor before diving if you tick conditions such as these:

  • asthma, wheezing or congested airways in the past 12 months that limit exercise, or other lung problems
  • heart problems, heart surgery or medicine for a heart condition
  • diabetes, including gestational diabetes in the past 12 months
  • ear disease or ear surgery, hearing loss or balance problems, sinus surgery in the past six months or repeated sinusitis
  • eye surgery in the past three months
  • fits, fainting or blackouts, a head injury with loss of consciousness in the past five years, or recurring migraines
  • any surgery in the past 12 months
  • frequent acid reflux, or treatment for panic attacks, depression or another mental health condition

The form has the full list, so read it yourself a week or two before the trip.

Three more things matter:

  • Pregnancy: the form says plainly that if you are pregnant, or trying to become pregnant, do not dive. Divers Alert Network reaches the same conclusion.
  • Alcohol: Divers Alert Network is clear that alcohol and diving are not compatible. Its advice is no more than two drinks the night before, and none on the dive day until the diving is over.
  • How you feel on the day: a cold, blocked ears, a stomach upset or a bad night can rule out a dive even if your form is clear. Read who should skip a dive before you travel.

Can non-swimmers do a try dive?

Yes, in most cases. PADI does not ask for a swim test for a try dive; it asks that you are comfortable in water and able to keep yourself at the surface. Karnataka Tourism also says Netrani's activities are open to people who cannot swim. Your instructor stays beside you from start to finish, and the dive team decides on the day whether you are ready.

What matters is calm, not swimming strokes. If putting your face in the sea makes you panic, start with snorkelling in a life jacket. A full certification course is different: it includes the 200 m swim test described above. Our sister site has a fuller guide to scuba for non-swimmers in Murudeshwar.

What can you do at Netrani if you cannot dive?

  • Snorkel. From ₹2,200 per guest, with a life jacket and a guide, on the same morning boat as the divers. The snorkelling vs scuba comparison helps you decide.
  • Take the boat trip. A private boat from ₹18,000 for six people lets a family with grandparents or small children go out at their own pace while others dive or snorkel.
  • Stay on the beach. Murudeshwar's beach has rides and boating for those who would rather stay near the shore; see Murudeshwar water sports and boating.

Questions travellers ask

Can a 60 year old do scuba diving?

Yes, if you are healthy. PADI sets no upper age, but over-45s answer extra heart-risk questions on the medical form, and some answers need a doctor's evaluation. Sort that out before you travel, not on the beach.

Is it safe for a 10 year old to scuba dive?

It can be, with the right instructor and a willing child. PADI allows try dives from 10, with tighter group limits when 10 and 11 year olds are diving. If your child is frightened on the day, let them snorkel instead.

What is the minimum age to go scuba diving in India?

Dive centres in India follow the standards of their training agency. Under PADI, try dives and the Junior Open Water course start at 10, and pool-only kids' sessions start at 8. Karnataka Tourism also gives 10 as the minimum for Netrani, subject to operator policy.

Can I scuba dive during my periods?

Yes. PADI says there is no physical reason not to dive while menstruating; it is a matter of personal comfort. If cramps or headaches are bad, snorkelling or another morning may feel better.

Do I need a medical certificate for a try dive?

Usually not. You fill in the diver medical questionnaire, and you need a doctor's signature only if your answers call for one. If you know you have a condition on the list, take the form to your doctor before the trip rather than finding out on the beach.

Can my 8-year-old dive at Netrani?

Not in the sea. PADI's programmes for 8 and 9 year olds, such as Bubblemaker, take place in a pool and go no deeper than 2 m. Tell the desk your child's age and we will confirm whether snorkelling suits them on that trip.
