Snorkeling vs Scuba Diving at Netrani: Which One Suits You?

By Netrani Desk · Updated 16 November 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answers

  • Snorkelling: you float at the surface in a life jacket for about an hour; it starts from ₹2,200 per guest.
  • Scuba: a guided try dive below the surface with an instructor, usually 15 to 40 minutes underwater, quoted per date.
  • Non-swimmers can snorkel in a life jacket; a try dive has no swim test but needs comfort in water, age 10+ and a medical form.
  • Divers and snorkellers usually share the same morning boat; a private boat starts from ₹18,000 for six people.

Snorkelling and scuba diving at Netrani start the same way: an early boat from Murudeshwar beach, about 19 km out to the island. The difference is what happens when you get there. Snorkellers float face down at the surface in a life jacket and watch the reef below, for about an hour. Scuba divers go under with an instructor on a guided try dive, usually for 15 to 40 minutes.

Snorkelling is the easier and cheaper choice. It starts from ₹2,200 per guest, needs no swimming and suits most of the family. Scuba is quoted per date and asks more of you: age 10 or above, a medical questionnaire, and enough calm to breathe underwater. If you are healthy and curious and can only do one, scuba shows you much more of the reef.

What is the same for both?

A lot of the day is shared, whichever you choose:

  • the early reporting on Murudeshwar beach and the same boat out, about 19 km and roughly 45 to 90 minutes each way
  • the open-sea crossing, so seasickness is just as likely either way
  • no landing on the island; everyone stays on or around the boat
  • the season, roughly October to May, and the morning check of the sea, which can move both activities to another day
  • the return to Murudeshwar in the afternoon

So the choice is only about your time in the water, not about the travel or the timings. The boat timings page explains the reporting window.

What do you see from the surface, and what from below?

From the surface you look down on the top of the reef: coral spread over the rocks, schools of fish moving between them and, on lucky days, a turtle. When the water is clear the view is good, because visibility at Netrani reaches 15 to 30 m in good conditions.

Scuba puts you level with the reef. You see under ledges and into the cracks where moray eels hide, groupers and snappers up close, and fish all around you instead of below you. One thing surprises first-timers: colours change as you go down. Red is the first colour to fade, within the first few metres, so the reef looks bluer to your eye than it does in photos taken with a light.

What if you can't swim or feel nervous?

Both are open to non-swimmers. For snorkelling, the life jacket keeps you afloat, and all you do is breathe through the snorkel with your face in the water. If anything feels wrong, you lift your head.

For a try dive, PADI's standard is that you do not need to be a strong swimmer, but you must be comfortable in water and able to keep yourself at the surface. Your instructor stays beside you from start to finish, and the dive team decides on the day whether you are ready. If deep water frightens you, there is no shame in snorkelling this time and diving on a later trip.

Nerves are normal either way. The difference is that underwater you cannot simply lift your head; you signal your instructor and come up slowly together, which is why the briefing matters. Our safety guide explains the rules that keep a try dive calm.

How do age and health change the choice?

This is where the two differ most. A try dive needs you to be 10 or older, which is PADI's minimum, to fill in the diver medical questionnaire and, if some answers call for it, to bring a doctor's signature. Pregnant women should not dive at all. A cold or blocked ears can rule out scuba on the day, because you have to clear your ears as you go down; at the surface there is no such pressure change, though you should still tell the crew how you feel.

For snorkelling, tell us the ages of any children and the crew will confirm what suits each one. The details on ages, over-45s and the medical form are in the Netrani scuba age limit guide.

Can a family do both on the same boat?

Yes. On a shared trip, divers and snorkellers usually go out on the same boat, and the crew takes the divers down in small groups while the others snorkel or rest on deck. Parents can dive while the rest of the family snorkels, as long as the crew is happy with the plan.

So which should you pick?

Choose snorkelling if:

  • you are under 10, pregnant, or still waiting for a doctor's clearance
  • you have a cold or blocked ears on the day
  • the idea of breathing underwater makes you anxious
  • you want a lighter, cheaper morning with the whole family together

Choose scuba if:

  • you are 10 or older, healthy and comfortable in water
  • you want to see the reef at eye level, not from above
  • underwater photos matter to you
  • you want to find out whether a dive course is for you

Anyone who wants a thrill without going underwater can add the beach rides in Murudeshwar; see water sports and boating in Murudeshwar.

Questions travellers ask

Is snorkeling easier than scuba diving?

Yes, for most people. Snorkelling needs only a mask, snorkel, fins and a life jacket, and the briefing takes a few minutes. Scuba adds a full set of gear, clearing your ears on the way down and a medical questionnaire, though on a try dive the instructor handles most of the technical side.

How risky is snorkelling at Netrani?

It is the gentler option, but it is still the open sea. Keep the life jacket on, stay close to the guide and the boat, wear sunscreen, and come out as soon as you feel tired, cold or unwell.

Can I snorkel and scuba dive on the same trip?

Often, yes, because both happen from the same boat at the island. Snorkelling before or after your dive depends on time and the sea that day, so mention it when you book and the desk will plan for it.

Do I need to know swimming for snorkelling at Netrani?

No. You wear a life jacket, float face down and breathe through the snorkel, with a guide nearby. Being relaxed in water helps a lot, so tell the crew if you feel nervous.

Which is better for underwater photos?

Scuba, because the camera can be level with you, the fish and the coral. Snorkelling photos are mostly taken from above. On our pricing page, underwater photos and videos are an add-on from ₹450.

Who is not allowed to scuba dive?

Children under 10, anyone pregnant, and anyone whose medical questionnaire answers need a doctor's sign-off they do not yet have. A cold, blocked ears or a recent illness can also rule out a dive on the day; see who should skip a dive.
