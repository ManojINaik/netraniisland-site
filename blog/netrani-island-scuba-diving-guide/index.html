Netrani Island Scuba Diving: A First-Timer's Guide to the Dive Day

Scuba diving at Netrani Island starts on Murudeshwar beach. You board a boat early in the morning and ride about 19 km out to the island, roughly 45 to 90 minutes depending on the boat and the sea. There you dive from the anchored boat with an instructor beside you. No certificate is needed for a first dive.

What you do need: to be 10 or older, to answer a medical questionnaire honestly, and to be comfortable in water. Most first-timers spend 15 to 40 minutes underwater, depending on how relaxed they are and what the sea is doing. Dives run roughly from October to May. The scuba price is quoted for your date rather than fixed, and the desk confirms it on WhatsApp before you pay anything.

Boats leave from the beach itself, so be ready to get your feet wet while boarding. Keep the morning simple: a light breakfast, water, sunscreen and a dry change of clothes. Our first-dive checklist covers the night before and boat nerves, the packing list keeps your bag light, and the boat timings page explains the reporting window.

The crossing is open sea, so expect some rolling. If boats make you queasy, tell the crew when you board and sit where you can see the horizon. Nobody lands on the island: Netrani is uninhabited and tourists are not allowed ashore, so the whole trip happens on and around the boat.

At the dive spot you usually get in by sitting on the side of the boat and rolling backwards, with your instructor already in the water. You float at the surface, practise breathing through the mouthpiece, and go down slowly with the instructor once you feel settled. Coming back up is just as slow.

How deep will you go, and for how long?

PADI's rules for a try dive, which it calls Discover Scuba Diving, allow a maximum of 12 m in open water, and PADI is clear that you are not taken deeper than you are comfortable with. The sea around Netrani runs from about 6 m to around 40 m deep, so there is shallow reef for beginners and deeper water for certified divers.

Time underwater is usually 15 to 40 minutes. Slow, calm breathing makes the air last longer; nervous breathing uses it up quickly. It also helps if your ears clear easily. Think of the package as a half-day sea outing, with the dive as the highlight rather than the whole day.

Scientists measured the water around Netrani at 26 to 30 °C in their October and April surveys, so the thin wetsuit that comes with the gear is plenty.

What will you see underwater?

Netrani is a rocky reef. Most of its coral grows spread over the rock, with some large rounded colonies, so do not expect tall branching coral. Fish are the main show. A 2006 survey by marine scientists counted 92 fish species around the island, and later counts found triggerfish, damselfish, fusiliers, wrasses, surgeonfish, sweetlips and parrotfish the most common. There are many kinds of grouper and snapper, plus butterflyfish, moray eels tucked into the cracks, and barracuda passing through on some days.

Turtles turn up now and then. A juvenile whale shark was recorded during that 2006 survey, but treat anything that big as a rare bonus. On good days you can see 15 to 30 m through the water, the range scientists measured in May 2006; after rough weather it can be much less. The marine life guide and the water clarity guide go further on both.

Who can do a try dive?

  • Age: 10 and above is PADI's minimum for a try dive. Operators can set their own limits, so tell us every diver's age.
  • Health: everyone fills in a diver medical questionnaire. Some answers, such as heart or breathing problems or recent surgery, need a doctor's signature before you dive. Pregnant women should not dive.
  • Swimming: there is no swim test for a try dive, but you should be comfortable in water and able to keep yourself at the surface. The dive team makes the final call.

Children, older divers, non-swimmers and the medical form are covered in the Netrani scuba age limit guide. If you have a cold, blocked ears or a recent illness, read who should skip a dive before you travel.

When is the diving season?

Netrani dives run roughly from October to May. Every monsoon the Uttara Kannada district administration bans sea water sports, scuba included; the 2026 ban ran from 1 June to 31 August, and the dates change each year. Even in season the crew checks the sea every morning. If it is not safe, we move you to the next workable day at no extra cost.

Already certified, or want to get certified?

Certified divers can ask for fun dives, which can go deeper than a try dive within the limits of your training: 18 m for PADI Open Water and 30 m for Advanced Open Water. Bring your certification card or app, and tell us roughly how many dives you have logged.

If a try dive leaves you wanting more, ask about a course. PADI lets a Discover Scuba dive count towards the Open Water course if the dive centre knows in advance and you start the course within 12 months.

Questions travellers ask

What is the cost of scuba diving in Netrani Island?

There is no fixed ticket price for scuba. The desk quotes it for your date after checking instructor availability, how many first-timers are in your group, the sea window and any add-ons such as photos. Published rates for snorkelling, private boats and add-ons are on the pricing page.

Which month is best for scuba diving in Netrani Island?

Dives run roughly from October to May, and the boats stop in the monsoon. Within the season the sea changes week to week, so a calm, clear day cannot be promised months ahead. Our guide to the best time to visit Netrani explains how to choose dates.

Can we stay on Netrani Island or land there?

No. The island is uninhabited and tourists are not allowed ashore, so every trip stays on the boat and returns to Murudeshwar the same day. Most divers spend the night before in Murudeshwar; see where to stay for Netrani.

Do I need a scuba certificate to dive at Netrani?

No. First-timers join a guided try dive with an instructor and need no certificate. Certified divers can ask for fun dives; mention your certification level and logged dives when you enquire.

Can I fly home on the same day after diving?

Divers Alert Network advises waiting at least 12 hours after a single dive before flying, and at least 18 hours after several dives or several days of diving. If you are flying out of Mangaluru or Goa, book the flight for the next day.

Can someone in my group come along without diving?

Yes. Non-divers can snorkel from the same boat, and families who want their own schedule can take a private boat. Tell the desk if anyone only wants the ride, and we will suggest the best way to fit them in.

How long does the whole Netrani scuba trip take?

Plan on most of the day: early reporting, the boat out and back, the briefing, the dive and some waiting while others dive. Boats usually return in the afternoon, so do not book a train or a long drive straight after.
